# Self-check: chapter 27, Directing the forecast

The three questions a good forecaster asks in this situation. A bad answer to any one of them is a reason to stop and fix the work before reporting.

1. **Which parts of the question have data to model, and which need judgment inputs, and did I keep them apart?**
   A bad answer looks like this: Letting a language model judge a number the data can compute is the chapter's central error.

2. **Is there a second route built from different evidence, and does the gap between routes tell me something?**
   A bad answer looks like this: Two routes fed the same inputs agreeing is one route counted twice.

3. **Are the judgment inputs listed with their sources, and is the scoring date set?**
   A bad answer looks like this: A launch forecast whose inputs cannot be traced cannot be improved when the actuals arrive.
