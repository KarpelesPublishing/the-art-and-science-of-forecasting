# Self-check: chapter 21, Inventory and intermittent demand

The three questions a good forecaster asks in this situation. A bad answer to any one of them is a reason to stop and fix the work before reporting.

1. **Is demand intermittent, and did I score forecasts on a scale-free error rather than MAE on zeros?**
   A bad answer looks like this: A forecast of zero has an excellent MAE on a slow mover and empties the shelf.

2. **Does the order-up-to level cover lead time plus review period at the stated service level, in a simulation?**
   A bad answer looks like this: A service level asserted from a formula and never simulated is a hope.

3. **Did I measure fill rate and cycle service separately, and report the cost of both errors?**
   A bad answer looks like this: A single service number hides which error the policy is making.
